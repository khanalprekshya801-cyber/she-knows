🚨 Violence Against Women

Let's talk about it. No scary textbook language, no pretending it doesn't happen — just things every girl deserves to know.

🏠 Home

Okay, so what actually counts as violence?

When we hear the word "violence," we sometimes imagine only physical attacks. But it's much bigger than that. Violence can look different from situation to situation — and some forms aren't visible at all.

👊 Physical Violence

Let's keep it simple.

Physical violence is when someone deliberately uses physical force to hurt another person. That can include hitting, kicking, pushing, choking or other actions that cause physical harm.

And yes, it can happen anywhere.

Home, school, work, relationships, public places — violence isn't limited to one particular place or type of relationship.

⚠️ Sexual Violence

Your body is yours.

Sexual violence includes unwanted sexual contact, sexual assault, rape and other forms of sexual abuse. Nobody gets to ignore someone's boundaries or force them into something they don't want.

Consent matters.

Consent means freely agreeing. If someone is pressured, threatened, forced or unable to give meaningful consent, that's not the same thing as freely saying yes.

🧠 Emotional & Psychological Violence

Not every wound is visible.

Constant humiliation, threats, intimidation, manipulation or deliberately making someone feel worthless can be emotionally abusive.

"But they didn't hit me."

That doesn't automatically mean everything is okay. Someone can seriously affect another person's sense of safety and wellbeing without ever physically hurting them.

🏠 Domestic Violence

It can happen inside a relationship.

Domestic violence can involve physical, sexual, emotional or financial abuse within a family or intimate relationship.

Control is a warning sign.

Constantly controlling someone's money, isolating them from people they trust, threatening them or monitoring everything they do can also be abusive behaviour.

💰 Economic Violence

Money can be used as control too.

Someone might take another person's earnings, stop them from working, control their bank account or deliberately make them financially dependent.

It's still abuse.

Just because there isn't a bruise doesn't mean there isn't harm. Financial control can make it much harder for someone to make her own choices or leave an unsafe situation.

📱 Online & Digital Violence

Yes, the internet counts too.

Not all violence happens face-to-face. Online harassment, threats, cyberstalking, impersonation and sharing someone's private information without permission can all be forms of digital abuse.

Your screen doesn't make it less serious.

A threatening message is still threatening. Someone repeatedly tracking or harassing you online isn't something you simply have to "put up with."

🚶 Harassment & Stalking

Harassment

Repeated unwanted comments, messages or behaviour can become harassment, especially when it makes someone feel threatened, intimidated or unsafe.

Stalking

Stalking can involve repeatedly following, monitoring or contacting someone when that attention isn't wanted. Everyone deserves to feel safe going about their day.

💜 How Can This Affect Someone?

It isn't "just one thing."

Violence can affect someone's physical health, mental wellbeing, relationships, education, work and everyday sense of safety.

And it can affect everyone around them.

Families, friends, classmates and entire communities can be affected too. That's why talking about violence isn't about blaming victims — it's about understanding the problem and taking it seriously.

🤝 How Do People Respond? — A Real Example

🇮🇳 The Jamui, Bihar Case

In September 2026, a teenage girl and a teenage boy in Jamui, Bihar, were reportedly stopped and harassed by a group while returning from coaching classes. Videos of the incident later spread online and led to widespread outrage.

Police registered a case and began identifying people seen in the videos. Several arrests were subsequently reported.

👮 What did the police do?
Police registered an FIR and investigated the people identified from the videos. Multiple arrests were reported, and a special investigation team was formed.

📢 How did people react?
The video caused a lot of anger and concern online. Politicians, women's rights bodies and members of the public called for action and accountability.

🏘️ What were some local reactions?
Interviews with some local residents showed that reactions were not all the same. Some comments focused on questioning why the teenagers had gone to that location and reflected a form of "moral policing."

And honestly, this is something worth talking about. A girl being somewhere, being with a friend, taking pictures, or simply being outside does not make harassment or violence her fault.

👩 What did the accused's family say?
The mother of the main accused publicly said that she believed her son was innocent and asked the government to give him another chance. She also made allegations about the police action against him.

Those statements are her claims. They should not be presented as proven facts unless they are established through the proper legal process.

🏛️ What did officials say?
The incident also led to political and official reactions. Some public figures strongly condemned what happened, while one Bihar political leader's description of the incident as "isolated" or "minor" drew criticism. Bihar minister Shreyasi Singh also faced backlash after remarks about "moral policing" and later said her words had been taken out of context.

💜 What happened to the girl's family?
The girl's mother later said the family received support from the Bihar government and administration. She said the Chief Minister assured them of her daughter's safety and education.

⚖️ What is the legal situation?
The case has involved arrests and an ongoing investigation. Because the legal process is still developing, we should be careful not to describe anyone as legally guilty unless a court has established that.

💗 One thing SHE KNOWS wants you to remember:

Sometimes after a case like this, people start asking, "Why was she there?" or "Why did she go with him?"

Those questions should never become excuses for someone else's alleged actions. Responsibility for harassment or violence belongs with the person who chooses to commit it.
